Warning Signs to Watch For

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A burning smell or a discolored access panel near the thermostat
  • A breaker that trips when the heater calls for heat
  • No hot water at all from any faucet
  • Tap water much hotter than the thermostat setting
  • A short burst of hot water, then nothing but tepid
  • Water dripping from an element gasket or pooling under the tank
  • Popping, sizzling, or crackling sounds from the tank
  • A heater that works after a reset, then quits again

Two things make electric water heater repair trickier than it looks: the power involved and the overlap between symptoms. These heaters run on 240-volt circuits, and the thermostats and elements are wired to terminals that stay live unless the breaker is off and verified. On the diagnostic side, a heater that will not heat might have a failed element, a bad thermostat, a tripped reset, or a wiring problem, and a reset that keeps tripping is a warning, not an annoyance. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Have the sacrificial anode inspected every three to five years
  • Lift the T&P valve lever yearly to make sure it opens and reseats
  • In hard water areas, a softener or low-watt-density elements can extend element life
  • Drain a few gallons from the bottom of the tank periodically to control sediment
  • If the reset button trips, have it checked rather than pressing it over and over
  • Make sure water flows steadily from a hot tap before the heater's power goes back on
  • Keep the thermostats set around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
