Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Carmel

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year if their water is hard.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
