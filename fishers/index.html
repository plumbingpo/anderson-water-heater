Keeping Your Fishers Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.
