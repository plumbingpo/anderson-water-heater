No Surprises

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Anderson Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing for every home, covering everything from a burned-out heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

The trouble with water heater failures is that many of them develop out of sight. Hard-water minerals build a crust on the tank floor, the anode rod gets eaten away gradually, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or a small drip appears at the base, have it checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of homeowners ask us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), replacing on your own schedule beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your unit has left.

Maintenance Plans

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service prevents failures before they start, clears out sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Anderson Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your actual heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a look at the burner and flue on gas models, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

Many homes do best with a yearly flush, and homes with hard water may need a visit every six months. The schedule flexes with the age of your heater, how hard your household works it, and local water quality, which determines how fast sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule pays off. A clean tank heats water faster and more quietly, a healthy anode rod keeps rust from attacking the steel, and a working relief valve is ready to vent pressure if something goes wrong. Most families find that annual service costs far less than replacing a water heater even one year early.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can hold when the incoming water is cold. That's why we never just copy the gallon rating of the old heater. Instead, we look at how many people live in the home, the number of bathrooms, plus large draws such as a jetted tub or high-capacity washer.

Picking a new water heater is about more than tank size. Our technician will compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, laying out operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, and a properly sized tank can be the simpler, lower-cost choice. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

A cold tap where hot water should be, a burner that refuses to light, a tank full of sediment, a slow drip from the relief valve: every one of these problems has its own cause and calls for a different repair. Anderson Water Heater tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's an electric tank that trips its breaker, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube mixing cold water into the hot, or a tankless unit flashing error codes.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A bad element only cuts your hot water supply. A relief valve spitting water, on the other hand, signals that pressure or temperature inside the tank is too high, and a slow leak at the base can quietly ruin subfloors and drywall before anyone notices. Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. We come prepared to swap elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, install expansion tanks where pressure is the problem, and handle leak repair at the drain valve or fittings.
